# 01 Executive recommendation

The opportunity is not generic “AI education videos.” It is short, original stories that turn a surprising question into a satisfying visual explanation. The channel should be accessible to curious non-specialists, understandable on mute, and recognizably ours without becoming formulaic.

| Thesis               | Implication                                                                                                                               |
|----------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Audience opportunity | Massive Shorts distribution infrastructure exists, but platform-wide views do not measure demand for our niche \[1\].                     |
| Competitive position | Compete on verified visual explanation, narrative design, original motion grammar and repeatable production—not cheap volume.             |
| Launch proof         | Build one high-quality math story; then publish a 12-video, four-series validation slate; collect seven-day observations where available. |
| Scale rule           | Only expand to the conditional 48-original study once quality, reliability, rights and unit economics gates pass.                         |
| Commercial scope     | Owned-channel traction tests content interest; paid creator/studio demand must be validated separately.                                   |

# 02 Niche definition and why it is attractive

# 2.1 Positioning

Channel promise: “Fascinating things made understandable through original AI animation.” Editorial formulation: entertainment first, science and mathematics underneath, accurate payoff at the end.

| **AUDIENCE HOOK** “Wait—how does that work?” is the emotional entry point. Equations and technical detail should arrive only after the viewer understands why the question matters. |
|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|

## 2.2 Market signals vs hypotheses

| Evidence we can substantiate                                                                           | What we cannot infer                                                                  |
|--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| YouTube reported Shorts averaging 200 billion daily views in its 2026 CEO letter \[1\].                | A new STEM channel will receive a particular distribution level or become viral.      |
| YouTube Studio exposes shown-in-feed, viewed-vs-swiped, views and subscriber changes for Shorts \[2\]. | A single metric fully measures explanation quality or unique audience reach.          |
| YouTube explicitly disallows repetitive, mass-produced inauthentic content from monetization \[3\].    | A visibly AI-generated video is necessarily disallowed; originality and value matter. |

Conclusion: attractive reach surface plus differentiated creative thesis, but niche-specific demand and competitiveness remain hypotheses. We should measure our own channel rather than treat platform-wide statistics or established creators as a forecast.

# 03 Audience segments and viewing motivations

| Audience segment               | Trigger / job                               | Winning content                                                | Potential action                |
|--------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------|
| Curiosity-first scrollers      | Surprising visual or counterintuitive claim | What-if demonstrations, impossible-looking patterns            | Rewatch or share                |
| Students and lifelong learners | A confusing concept made intuitive          | Geometric proofs, mechanics, probability                       | Save or follow series           |
| Tech/AI enthusiasts            | Understand invisible systems quickly        | Algorithms, neural-network flows, internet mechanics           | Follow for recurring explainers |
| Educators and creators         | Clear diagrams suitable for sharing         | Accurate, reusable teaching concepts                           | Share with learners             |
| Global viewers on mute         | Immediate comprehension without audio       | Strong first-frame visuals, readable subtitles, stable symbols | Complete / revisit              |

Behavioral hypothesis: surprise earns the first second; object continuity and visual cause-and-effect sustain attention; a resolved mystery produces the payoff. Treat these as creative testing hypotheses, not universal psychological laws.

# 05 Editorial portfolio: topics × formats

Preserve the PRD-defined four series and first 12-original slate. The proposed subject-mix percentages below are an exploratory tagging hypothesis, not a replacement for the series allocation.

| Series (PRD)              | Count / 12 | Typical story arc                                         | Example                                 |
|---------------------------|------------|-----------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| What If                   | 4          | Premise → assumptions → consequences → bounded answer     | What if gravity were twice as strong?   |
| Wait, How Does That Work? | 4          | Familiar observation → mechanism → satisfying explanation | Why does a spinning top resist falling? |
| Solve This                | 2          | Fair challenge → visual clues → proof / reveal            | Why do odd numbers make a square?       |
| Inside the Machine        | 2          | Visible action → hidden process → traced flow             | What happens when you press Send?       |

Proposed topical exploration tags: ~40% visual mathematics and patterns; ~35% physics and everyday science; ~25% puzzles, technology and AI systems. A single story can have multiple tags; do not force exact quotas on 12 videos.

# 06 Creative grammar and quality standard

## 6.1 Repeatable 30–60-second narrative

| Beat          | Suggested duration | What viewer should experience                                                |
|---------------|--------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Hook          | 0–3 s              | A compelling question and unmistakable visual contradiction or payoff teaser |
| Setup         | 3–10 s             | Minimal context; establish objects and persistent color meanings             |
| Demonstration | 10–30 s            | One visible causal transformation per beat; evidence agrees with motion      |
| Payoff        | 30–45 s            | Reveal the mechanism or proof promised in the opening                        |
| Closure       | final 3–8 s        | A memorable line/visual, with unobtrusive brand signature                    |

Time ranges are starting design parameters, not proven ideal durations. Retiming is necessary after measuring real voice segments and previewing footage.

## 6.2 Required creative rules

- Original first frame; never begin with a long logo or generic title screen.

- Use dark canvas, accessible contrast, persistent color/labels, and stable object identity; never merely recreate Lingualin screenshots.

- Every animation must advance an explanation; cinematic shots support atmosphere but must not pretend to be scientific simulation.

- Captions, mathematical notation and safety margins must be legible on a phone with sound muted.

- Material scientific statements must have a claim ledger and documented review; illustrative assumptions must be explicit.

- Measure first-video effort, rendering time, revisions, cost, and subjective comprehension—not only view counts.

# 07 Tools and applications: production strategy

Use applications to accelerate shot creation, not as the identity of the channel. Visual accuracy and reusable animation remain the differentiated product capability.

| Role                               | Recommended applications                                | Status for OpportunityOS                                                              |
|------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Precise 2D/math                    | Manim Community; Remotion                               | Proposed core; Remotion is source-available with license conditions \[4\]             |
| Local/open-source AI video GUI     | LTX Desktop; ComfyUI                                    | Evaluate as experimental cinematic adapter \[5,6\]                                    |
| Rapid cinematic reference          | Google Flow; Krea; Dreamina; PixVerse                   | Compare output quality; free tiers, rights and API paths require current verification |
| Voice, captions, encoding          | TTS provider adapter; FFmpeg                            | Select voice from rights, pronunciation and latency tests                             |
| Workflow, governance and artifacts | FastAPI; Temporal candidate; PostgreSQL; object storage | Architecture proposal per PRD, not verified implementation                            |

LTX Desktop can generate locally on supported hardware, with device-specific restrictions and optional charged API modes \[5\]. ComfyUI is an open-source GUI and programmable workflow backend \[6\]. Open-source application licensing does not automatically imply that every model weight, soundtrack or output is unrestricted.

## 7.1 Visual routing rule

| Scene demand                                      | Default route                                              | Use generative video when…                                        |
|---------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Equations, counts, mathematical proof             | Deterministic Manim/Remotion + reviewed calculations       | Only for non-authoritative aesthetic cutaways                     |
| Mechanism, process, information flow              | Remotion diagrams / verified simulation                    | A scene needs an atmospheric establishing shot                    |
| Natural-looking environments, speculative what-if | Cinematic app/adapter + deterministic explanatory overlays | The depiction is clearly illustrative and scientifically reviewed |
| Final production                                  | Remotion/FFmpeg + narration/captions + QA                  | Never skip approval, rights or factual checks                     |

# 08 Launch, experiment and traction roadmap

## Phase 0 — Nov–Dec 2026: feasibility

Complete M0 discovery, initial visual brand and the M1 six-family animation feasibility sample. Deliver the 30–45-second “Why do odd numbers build a square?” video with verified counts, readable captioning, narration alignment and a scene-specific revision. Compare manual effort against a basic editor workflow. This proves production feasibility, not audience traction.

## Phase 1 — January 2027: small channel launch

Launch with three to five high-quality, reviewed originals and manual publication if integrations are not ready. Observe actual platform results; do not block launch on full automation or access to Instagram APIs. Record original file, channel URL, publication date, evidence, human effort and usage cost.

## Phase 2 — PRD R2: twelve-video quality gate

Expand to 12 approved originals across the 4/4/2/2 series allocation. Each must pass factual, rights, originality, audio, video and publication-evidence review. The PRD proposes at least 10/12 without code-level repair as an internal production reliability threshold, pending formal adoption. This slate is separate from the later growth study.

## Phase 3 — Conditional 90-day growth study

Only after R2 passes: publish up to 48 additional originals in 12 weekly slates (up to four per week), adapted for YouTube and eligible Instagram distribution. At most 96 uploads if every original is separately approved and posted to both platforms. Reserve the tail of the 90-day study for observation and analysis. The stretch target is 1,000,000 cumulative platform-reported organic views for study uploads—an ambition, not a forecast.

| Study window | Max new originals | Main learning objective                                          |
|--------------|-------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Days 1–30    | 16                | Compare hooks, subject families and explanatory pacing           |
| Days 31–60   | 16                | Repeat promising patterns without eliminating exploration        |
| Days 61–90   | 16                | Optimize formats and production consistency; consolidate results |

# 09 Measurement architecture and decision rules

The scorecard should compare videos within the same platform, age window, format and duration band. YouTube changed the Shorts view definition on 31 March 2025: starts/replays count as views, while engaged views remain a distinct indicator \[7\]. Therefore views alone cannot stand in for sustained attention.

| Metric                                         | What it suggests                           | Interpretation guardrail                                   |
|------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------|
| Shown in feed / exposure                       | Distribution opportunity                   | Dependent on platform availability; not demand by itself   |
| Chose to view vs swiped away                   | First-frame relevance and hook strength    | Use same platform/time window; not universal quality proof |
| Average view duration / % viewed               | Attention through story                    | Loops, length and platform definitions affect comparison   |
| Completion and replays (if available)          | Satisfying pacing or repeat appeal         | Not direct proof of comprehension                          |
| Shares / saves                                 | Perceived usefulness or remarkable quality | Use eligible metric and actual denominator                 |
| Subscribers/followers per 1,000 eligible views | Series-level future interest               | Avoid over-attributing after a single viral event          |
| Cost and human minutes per approved video      | Repeatability and operational efficiency   | Include attempts, failed renders and review time           |

## 9.1 Experiment contract

Each published experiment records: content ID; platform; original and hook variant; pre-registration of audience question; opening/pacing/visual treatment; factual and animation verification; intended primary metric; comparable baseline or COLD_START; metric definitions; observation window (initial proposal: seven days); measured outcomes; confounders; next editorial decision.

## 9.2 Diagnose rather than chase vanity metrics

| Pattern                            | Hypothesis to investigate                           | Next controlled test                                         |
|------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| Low chose-to-view                  | Opening frame unclear or wrong topic fit            | New first 2 seconds with same core explanation               |
| Strong start, early retention drop | Setup too slow, jargon or confusing transformations | Shorten setup or simplify object animation                   |
| High completion, little sharing    | Not memorable or actionable                         | Stronger final reveal while preserving correctness           |
| High views, weak subscriber gain   | One-off topic outside channel promise               | Related episode with recognizable visual signature           |
| Low reach despite good retention   | Sample/distribution/topic limits                    | Cross-topic replication; avoid claiming platform suppression |

# 10 Prioritization, scoring and operating controls

The PRD proposes an editable Content Opportunity score, not a “virality probability”: broad curiosity 25%, visual payoff 25%, storytelling 20%, shareability 15%, originality and factual confidence 15%. Rate each 1–5, then calculate weighted score / 5 for a 0–100 prioritization number. Keep evidence confidence separate and allow operator override with rationale.

| Preflight gate         | Pass evidence                                                                                   |
|------------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Editorial feasibility  | Single clear question, non-misleading payoff, defined target audience                           |
| Scientific feasibility | Sources and assumptions reviewed; values/models verified where used                             |
| Animation feasibility  | Every requested action supported by bounded scene components or labeled cinematic illustration  |
| Rights and disclosure  | Asset/voice/music model terms documented; required synthetic media disclosure assessed          |
| Budget                 | Estimated model/GPU/TTS/revision cost within allowance                                          |
| Publication authority  | Exact approved video/metadata/destination; actual observed publication not confused with export |

# 11 Risks, dependencies and guardrails

| Risk                                  | Why it matters                                       | Mitigation / decision                                                 |
|---------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Viral-content overfitting             | One-hit reach gives false confidence                 | Repeated tests; median/percentiles; hold exploration share            |
| Mass-produced sameness                | Viewer fatigue and monetization risk \[3\]           | Original scripts, varied mechanisms, editorial sign-off               |
| Visually impressive but wrong science | Damages trust and understanding                      | Verified calculations; specialist review; label illustrative shots    |
| Copyright/licensing                   | Reused assets or model restrictions block publishing | Rights ledger and per-provider checks                                 |
| GPU/time cost growth                  | Unbounded attempts and revisions reduce viability    | Budget caps, approved regeneration, cache and record costs            |
| Platform access or metric changes     | Missing APIs may disrupt workflows                   | Manual publication fallback; version metric definitions               |
| High production workload              | January target may become unrealistic                | Three-to-five-video launch; scope-gated R2 expansion                  |
| Views without commercial demand       | Reach does not validate creator SaaS                 | Interview creators/studios and seek repeat-use paid pilots separately |
